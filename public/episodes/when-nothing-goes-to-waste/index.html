When Nothing Goes to Waste

What if a city never threw anything away?

Editorial perspectives: Dali + Sofia · Ethan — The Challenger

  • Vacuum waste collection, recycling robots and CO₂-storing concrete exist; the robot loop is a concept
  • Today and future possibilities

Follow what we throw away.

Every week trucks haul our trash out of town and most of it is never used again. Episode 007 imagines one loop under the streets: robot pods deliver parcels to every building, carry waste and broken things back to a recovery hub and send materials back to the makers.

Collection night: overflowing bins, torn bags on the sidewalk and a garbage truck blocking the street. Most of what it carries will never be used again. What if a town kept everything in use?

Dali connects the systems. Sofia explores the intelligence behind the robots. Ethan tests the trade-offs. These are the editorial perspectives through which we explore this question.

Trash already travels underground.

In parts of Stockholm, residents drop their trash into an inlet and a vacuum pipe carries it to a collection station, with far fewer trucks on the street.

Robots can take things apart.

Apple's recycling robot, Daisy, can dismantle about two hundred iPhones an hour and recover materials like cobalt for new batteries.

One loop under the streets.

Imagine small robot pods that deliver parcels and groceries to a locker in every building. On the way back they carry waste, packaging and broken things to a recovery hub at the edge of town, where robots sort and take everything apart.

Potential applications

Where could it make a difference?

Different capabilities, different conditions. The whole system has to work.

Ambition, tested

Ethan’s Reality Check

“But what are we missing?”

Tunnels are costly and far easier to build in a new district than under an old city; in Switzerland, planning for the Cargo sous terrain freight tunnels was paused in 2025 because its private funding was not secured. Most products are not yet designed to be taken apart and some waste will always leave town. The robot loop and recovery hub are an IMAGINE concept; zero waste is a direction, not a promise.

  • Tunnels are costly: can they be built anywhere but a new district? In Switzerland, the Cargo sous terrain freight tunnel plans were paused in 2025 because private funding was not secured.
  • Who designs products so they can be taken apart?
  • What happens to the waste that cannot come back?
  • Who owns the loop and pays for it?

How to read the visuals

As disclosed with the published video, this episode uses AI-generated and digitally created illustrations. The robot pods, the recovery hub and the city scenes are conceptual visuals, not footage of a real installation. The robot loop and recovery hub are an IMAGINE concept, not an existing project. Zero waste is a direction, not a promise; future possibilities are scenarios, not guaranteed outcomes.

Current tools. Experimental workflows. Imagined outcomes.

The capability behind the claim

  • Established

  • Established
    Robots that take products apart for recycling

  • Emerging
