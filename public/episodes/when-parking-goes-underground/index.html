When Parking Goes Underground

What if the best parking space is the one you never see?

Editorial perspectives: Dali · Ethan — The Challenger

  • Automated parking operating; loop pavilion is a concept
  • Today and future possibilities

The space we give to standing cars.

Surface parking takes up some of the most valuable land in our cities. Episode 004 imagines a glass pavilion on a city square: you drive in, step out, and the whole bay sinks below the street. Underground, a loop of ten self-moving bays keeps charging and brings your car back up, already facing the exit.

Every evening, curbs are lined with parked cars while drivers circle in search of a space. What if the cars could simply disappear below the street, and the space they leave behind went back to the people who live there?

Dali connects the systems. Ethan tests the trade-offs. These are the editorial perspectives through which we explore this question.

Drive in. Walk away.

A glass pavilion stands on a city square. You drive in on one side, step out through a sliding door, and the whole bay sinks below the street, car and all. The next morning your car rises in the other bay, already facing the exit.

Ten bays that balance each other.

Underground, a loop of ten bays turns like a vertical carousel. Because the bays hang in a loop, they balance one another, and three motors are enough to drive it. Charging continues while the bays rotate.

Streets given back to people.

Green for empty, red for occupied, managed from one control room. Above ground, the squares and curbs once filled with parked cars could be given back to trees, walkways and people.

Potential applications

Where could it make a difference?

Different capabilities, different conditions. The whole system has to work.

Automated parking

Mechanical and robotic systems already park cars without a driver in the garage.

Plate recognition

Entry and exit by license plate, as many garages already do today.

Charging while parked

Electric cars charge while they wait below the street.

Livable public space

Curbs and squares reclaimed for trees, walkways and terraces.

What exists today

Current Reality

Automated and mechanical parking systems already operate in cities today, and license plate recognition is standard at many parking garages.

What we can imagine

Future Possibility

Could a drive-in, walk-away pavilion with a self-moving underground loop free city squares for people while cars keep charging below?

Ambition, tested

Ethan’s Reality Check

“But what are we missing?”

The self-moving loop pavilion, its charging rail and the space figures shown are an IMAGINE concept, not an existing product. Digging costs, groundwater, fire safety and rescue access are real challenges that would need to be solved.

  • What do digging and construction cost compared with the land they free?
  • How are groundwater, fire safety and rescue access handled underground?
  • Can the loop return cars quickly enough at the busiest times?
  • Would fewer cars, shared mobility or better transit free the same space more cheaply?
