When Data Centers Heat Our Homes

What if the cloud kept you warm?

Editorial perspectives: Dali + Sofia · Ethan — The Challenger

  • Data-center heat reuse operating; data tower is a concept
  • Today and future possibilities

The heat we throw away.

Almost every watt a server uses ends up as heat. Most of it is blown into the sky while homes nearby burn gas to stay warm. Episode 005 follows the heat and imagines a neighborhood built around its own data center: servers on the lower floors, homes above and a warm-water loop to the blocks around it.

On a winter night, thousands of homes burn gas to stay warm while, just down the road, a data center blows its heat into the sky. What if that heat went into the homes instead?

Dali connects the systems. Sofia follows the data. Ethan tests the trade-offs. These are the editorial perspectives through which we explore this question.

Follow the heat.

Almost every watt a server uses ends up as heat. In 2024, data centers used about one and a half percent of the world's electricity and that share could double by 2030. Today most of their heat is blown into the sky.

It already works.

In Odense, Denmark, a data center sends its heat through heat pumps into the town's heating network and keeps about 9,000 homes warm. Near Helsinki, a much larger scheme is being built: from 2027, data centers there should supply about 40 percent of the heat for a quarter of a million people.

A neighborhood built around its data center.

Imagine servers on the lower floors and homes above, each with its own entrance. Warm water from the servers rises through the tower and out to the blocks around it. A small heat pump in every home does the rest, while summer heat is stored under the square for winter.

Potential applications

Where could it make a difference?

Different capabilities, different conditions. The whole system has to work.

District heating

Data-center heat already feeds town heating networks in Northern Europe.

Heat pumps

They lift low-temperature server heat to the temperature homes need.

Seasonal storage

Summer heat saved underground for the cold months.

Land for parks

No separate industrial site, so more of the neighborhood can stay green.

What exists today

Current Reality

Data-center heat already warms homes in Denmark, Finland and Sweden. EU and German rules now push large data centers to reuse their heat.

What we can imagine

Future Possibility

Could a neighborhood be designed around its own data center, so the cloud keeps its residents warm?

Ambition, tested

Ethan’s Reality Check

“But what are we missing?”

The data tower, its warm-water loop, the seasonal heat store and the Energy Port backup are an IMAGINE concept, not an existing project. A strong grid connection, safe separation of servers, batteries and homes and a backup heat source if the data center leaves are real challenges.

  • Is there enough grid capacity for a data center inside a neighborhood?
  • How are servers, batteries and homes safely separated?
  • Where does the heat come from if the data center leaves or shrinks?
  • Who pays for the heat network and who owns it?
